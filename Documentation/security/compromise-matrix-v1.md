# Compromise matrix and recovery runbooks v1

Status: Draft. Issue: [Identity #18](https://github.com/Cratis/Identity/issues/18). Owner review and operating commitments: [Identity #20](https://github.com/Cratis/Identity/issues/20). This is a proposed response contract, not evidence of implemented containment, completed rehearsals or production sign-off.

## Boundaries and matrix

A compromised process has the full authority of every key, credential and realm it can reach. Containment comes from authority it does **not** hold. The controls below describe the intended deployment, not a guarantee about an unverified installation. Inventory actual key reuse, replicas, secret-store access and hosted realms before choosing the recovery scope; broaden it when exposure cannot be bounded.

## Recovery rules shared by the runbooks

Use the draft [revocation protocol](../specs/identity-revocation-v1.md), especially §§3, 6–8: commit the scoped fence/epoch and outbox atomically, append durably to the independent fence log, and acknowledge only after both required durable commits. Preserve target ids, tombstones and retry identities. Review pending failures rather than treating a timeout as a successful revoke. Chronicle delivery is asynchronous; an eventually consistent projection is not revocation authority.

Revocation covers browser/on-behalf/runner sessions, OAuth authorizations and refresh families, delegation lineage, approvals and product-local PATs as applicable to the exposed authority. Product credential authorities must perform their own fences. Advance the applicable app/grant or realm epoch through a trusted authority and require affected verifiers to enforce it; a claim or rolled-back database counter alone does not invalidate anything. Do not invent a new wire claim or assume `jti` is a grant id. If trustworthy status/epoch correlation is unavailable, keep the affected routes denied.

Offline JWTs are not instantly revoked by rotating keys or revoking refresh families. Enforce authoritative status for sensitive work and stay closed until emergency rejection is proven across replicas or the [access-token profile](../specs/identity-access-token-v1.md)'s accepted lifetime plus skew has elapsed after the **last possible malicious issuance**. Confirm actual deployed bounds. Emergency compromised-key retirement is not routine rollover: no acceptance overlap for the compromised key; stage replacement trust only on clean components while traffic is denied. Bound and record any legitimate, uncompromised-key overlap from deployed envelope/token lifetime and cache rules; no duration is invented here.

If restoring state, apply revocation §6: prove contiguous independent fence-log coverage through the recovery barrier, reconcile outboxes and tombstones, and invalidate potentially rolled-back refresh families/approvals by trusted monotonic epoch unless independent consumption coverage proves safety. Missing coverage or uncertain verifier enforcement means not ready. A healthy process or rebuilt projection is insufficient.

For every case, record the exposure window from earliest plausible compromise through containment and confirmed denial, widening it if evidence is incomplete. Preserve restricted audit evidence without copying raw tokens, PATs, session handles, keys or DPoP proofs into events, tickets or communications. Logs should correlate non-secret credential/grant ids, realm, app, audience, tenant, key id, fence sequence and timestamps. Recovery does not undo committed operations; product owners investigate and remediate affected data separately.

The [verified-caller contract](../specs/arc-verified-caller-v1.md) requires independent product authorization and no unsigned fallback. The [DPoP profile](../specs/identity-dpop-v1.md) prevents bound-token replay only when the key and enforcing path remain trustworthy; it does not contain a compromised AuthProxy or issuer. An incident must not introduce a Bearer downgrade for bound tokens.

## Decision dependencies and acceptance

- **D13 — pending #15 (D13):** [shared products issuer-key risk](https://github.com/Cratis/Identity/issues/15). The shared Identity-host row and the products-realm portion of control-plane recovery include Direct and Studio together if they share signing authority. Edge-key and detached backend incidents do not acquire that blast radius merely because products share a realm; escalate if issuer authority is reachable. Acceptance must record products in scope, signing-key isolation, chosen token lifetime, rotation/revocation/re-authentication runbook, drill owner and date. Separate product realms/federation is the alternative; separate realms alone do not establish separate process authority. No shared products realm release while acceptance is pending.
- **D19 — pending #17 (D19):** [hosted Stage OAuth](https://github.com/Cratis/Identity/issues/17). The shared Identity-host row includes **all** Stage realms on the host under option B. Acceptance must name allowed realms/isolation tiers, acknowledge total shared-host exposure, require rotation of every served realm's keys, grant revocation and user re-authentication, and state when option C (per-Performance detached host) is required. Keep the products realm on its dedicated tier, not the shared Stage host. A backend incident escalates to host scope in compact/self-hosted profiles that embed issuer authority. D13 does not accept D19's customer-host risk, or vice versa; the recommendation of B is not a decision.
- **D17:** Design A §10 proposes per-app keys/audiences, v2 verification-only backends and AuthProxy treated like an issuer, with rehearsed rotation, app-session revocation and exposure-window audit review. The edge-key and AuthProxy rows depend on verifying these controls, not on assuming a single app is always the maximum scope. This document does not approve a shared edge key.
- **Owner role pending #20:** [bounded operational ownership](https://github.com/Cratis/Identity/issues/20). All roles above are proposed functions, not staffed assignments. Before release, record primary/coverage, security patch budget/escalation, conformance and OpenIddict upgrade owner, and first/recurring restore and rotation drill dates, including fence-log recovery. No patch time or drill interval has been agreed here. Use shared implementations, measure footprint/on-call load, and do not change Identity, AuthProxy, Ante and Arc runtime contracts incompatibly at the same time.

## Review and open points

Owner review in #20 and sign-off in [#14](https://github.com/Cratis/Identity/issues/14) remain required. Infrastructure #294 and #299 are rehearsal references, not evidence that any drill passed. Extend or create the explicitly missing rehearsal issues; supply the infrastructure-owned cluster response link before operational use.
