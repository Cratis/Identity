# Grant fences and refresh admission

A grant fence denies online refresh admission without waiting for Chronicle, a projection, a dispatcher, or OpenIddict cleanup. One majority-durable `grant-fences` document owns the revision, irreversible fence, current refresh entry identity, and source-fact delivery intents. Every replacement compares the document revision. Tombstones and delivery intents have no TTL.

Refresh allocates its successor identity before the grant CAS. That CAS consumes the presented identity and installs the successor identity against the same revision that revoke writes. If revoke commits first, refresh cannot install. If refresh commits first, revoke clears its installed successor and permanently denies subsequent admission. OpenIddict's later token-status update records reuse evidence, not the authoritative consume decision. Its failed redemption is explicitly rejected, unlike the stock handler's ignored failure. `FencedTokenStore` inserts the actual preallocated `ObjectId`; the pinned OpenIddict model exposes a settable `BsonId` and stock `CreateAsync` preserves it.

A non-current parent can still look valid in OpenIddict while the CAS winner updates its entry. Validation and the token endpoint let that known parent reach redemption, which rejects the loser and fences the family as reuse even before OpenIddict records redemption. Requests that read the same revision also detect the consumed parent after a lost CAS.

The winning request retains a scoped admission snapshot only after the CAS and successful OpenIddict redemption. A later fence does not reject its held response or successor insert: the request was ordered before that fence. The snapshot bounds the successor to the original family expiry and cannot authorize another request. The fence still denies that successor on next use, even when its insertion follows revocation cleanup.

A crash between the grant CAS and the successor insert can strand a family. Recovery never re-admits the consumed entry: the user must obtain a fresh grant. Initial creation and legacy-family repair atomically record the original family admission and expiry. Repair requires a live validated entry and authorization; missing, malformed, or unavailable authority/outbox state is not permission. During a rolling deployment an old-code replica can still issue through its old CAS; a successor not admitted by the new authority is denied and the user reauthenticates. Rolling back leaves unused grant documents, not new admission authority. Rotation does not slide the original absolute expiry or emit success telemetry facts.

Revocation atomically persists the tombstone and delivery intents first, runs the existing OpenIddict authorization/token cleanup, and confirms the exact immutable fence in the log before acknowledgement. A failure never acknowledges success or removes denial. Retries retain the fence identity, sequence, fact identities and reuse detection identity. RFC 7009 commits the fence before its built-in entry revocation; a retry must confirm a pending log before built-in `invalid_token` handling can become a success response.

## Durability boundary

The shipped registration uses `MongoIndependentFenceLog`, not `CoLocatedMongoFenceLog`. Provision it on a separate Mongo replica-set deployment with separate workload access and an independent backup policy. It must be excluded from **both** the operational and Chronicle backup/restore sets. A second database, connection alias, or different connection text on the same deployment is insufficient. Startup compares the two replica-set identities and refuses equal or unreadable identities. This is a protocol implementation, **not evidence of production B1 provisioning or a completed restore drill**.

Reservations and sequence allocation commit together in the independent log. Its contiguous watermark L includes filled restrictions, proven voids, and durable recovery-epoch intents; unresolved reservations block advancement. Cancellation and writer commit contend on the same operational grant document. Reconciliation first moves a reservation to Fencing, then atomically cancels its operational writer or recovers its already committed complete record. Only proven cancellation permits a void. An unreachable primary, timeout, or unknown outcome stays unresolved. Cancellation markers are also replayed after restore, so delayed old writers cannot revive voided work.

The operational checkpoint O and nonce are mirrored in independent custody by one leased recovery owner. The nonce is stable for that host incarnation; followers do not write competing nonces. Progress publishes independent evidence first and conditionally replaces the previously observed operational checkpoint, preserving evidence of a concurrent rollback. Recovery verifies every retained tombstone through the contiguous watermark L, including omissions above O, and replays the uncheckpointed tail. Normal polls do not cancel live reservations or require the allocated tail to close; pending startup recovery does not stop independent outbox delivery. Tombstones, cancellation markers, and immutable intent payloads have no TTL or v1 compaction. Restriction FactIds and reuse DetectionIds are derived from the original fence identity; replay preserves the stored payload, keys and occurrence time. C advances only after every intent in its prefix and its epoch facts are durably appended, followed by a keyed Chronicle publication checkpoint and independent confirmation. Chronicle restore resets delivery once per fence/restore epoch; bounded batches never count as complete replay.

The outbox worker recovers pending log confirmation before appending any restriction fact. It uses Chronicle's durable `AppendOnce` key `(FactId, EventTypeId)` and marks delivery afterward. Replaying after append but before that mark uses the same key. Unsupported keyed appends, conflicts, and errors hold delivery; there is no unkeyed fallback. Chronicle 19.111.0 exposes these APIs; its `AppendResult.IsSuccess` does not include idempotency conflicts, so the writer checks that flag separately. Deployments require a kernel advertising `SupportsIdempotentAppends`. The draft Chronicle #18 contract remains a dependency; revisit the append/conflict and restore wording when it lands.

## Provisioning and restore runbook

Bind the internal `Identity:FenceLog` section using approved secret injection for `ConnectionString`. Required non-secret settings are `Database`, an independently recorded `InstanceId`, and `MinimumSequence` from independent audit/monitoring. `MinimumSequence` is the last known allocated sequence and is checked against the complete reservation inventory, not just a high counter. Never lower it to make startup pass. Missing configuration, missing custody metadata, identity mismatch, truncation or unreadable deployment identity holds recovery closed; production never initializes an empty log automatically.

1. Have the infrastructure owner provision the independent replica set, workload permissions (including the read-only `replSetGetConfig` identity probe), unique reservation sequence index, and backup exclusion. The parent must track this provisioning and the uncovered recovery work in Infrastructure/Identity issues; no deployment is claimed here. Preserve log-instance identity, allocation bounds and backup/incarnation evidence independently of both consumers. Keep tombstones at least as long as all revoked grants and all consumer backup retention/restore windows; v1 does not delete them.
2. For a genuinely new realm only, the operator creates the `fence-custody` metadata document: `_id: "metadata"`, `instanceId` matching independently recorded custody, `allocated: NumberLong(0)`, `epoch: NumberLong(0)`. Supply a fresh restore declaration and independent evidence that no earlier state exists. Never create this marker to repair a lost/re-created log or copy it from a consumer backup. Lost custody needs independent recovery of the original complete log and external continuity proof, not an empty replacement.
3. Before restoring **any** consumer, quiesce all affected replicas and use routes. Preserve the independent log. Supply a unique opaque `RestoreId` and `RestoreKind` (`Operational`, `Chronicle`, or `Both`) to one recovery owner; other replicas wait without that declaration. A changed kind, completed declaration, or declaration reused by another host is refused. A live owner's retry resumes the same epoch. After an interrupted owner, use a fresh declaration, never reuse its identity. Chronicle-only rollback requires an explicit declaration; it cannot be inferred from operational watermarks alone.
4. Every start begins unready. The owner reconciles abandoned reservations, validates the entire inventory, replays denials and cancellation markers, and advances the independent epoch with a stable recovery fence/publication intent. Pre-restore refresh families are rejected, not upgraded. On Chronicle/Both restore it rebuilds delivery markers and requires successful keyed replay to L before readiness. Operational-only recovery does not wait on Chronicle delivery once operational restrictions and independent coverage are proven; durable epoch intents remain retryable.
5. **Do not set `RestrictionsReestablished: true` merely because grant replay passed.** It is an operator attestation, tied to that unique restore declaration, that every uncovered restriction has been independently reconstructed and every pre-epoch stateful capability invalidated across all affected authorities/verifiers. Automatic rollback detection always remains denied. After independent reconciliation, declare a fresh restore with this setting only when the accountable owners have proved the obligations below. Once complete, remove the declaration from subsequent normal starts; keep its durable custody history. Never clear a gate or reset an epoch directly.
6. Verify old-family denial, exact once-only source facts, current epoch enforcement at each verifier, key/client-secret retirement and readiness for every affected realm before routing traffic. This aligns with [shared Identity host recovery](../../../Documentation/security/runbooks/shared-identity-host.md): restore is not complete merely because grants replayed. Expose the registered `identity-restriction-recovery` health check on the host readiness endpoint, separately from liveness. Export meter `Cratis.Identity.Recovery`, gauge `identity_recovery_unready_seconds`; alert after **5 minutes** unready, investigate log/primary/append failures, and never time out into Ready. Worker retries are bounded to 5–60 seconds; reservation abandonment waits 30 seconds and still requires the cancellation handshake.

### Covered classes and remaining restore gates

Grant fences and refresh-family epochs retain their independent replay. Authorization codes, other token entries (including host-side access-token validation/introspection), authorizations and pending consent now carry an immutable creation epoch. The stamp is written in the same operational insert, preserved through OpenIddict property replacement and update, and never refreshed on an existing entry. Changing or explicitly removing it is rejected. An entry from an earlier epoch is denied even if its restored status appears valid. Missing stamps are accepted only at epoch zero for rollout safety; malformed stamps are denied. Every pre-restore code, token, authorization and pending consent therefore requires fresh authentication/authorization. No token claims or Public event contracts are added.

Signing keys come from the secret store and client secrets from read-only configuration, outside the operational/Chronicle restore set; restoring those sources themselves requires separate retirement reconciliation. If client/key retirement becomes event-backed, independent tombstone custody must be implemented before that coverage is retained. Legacy refresh bootstrap remains permanently disabled after a restore, even following explicit reconciliation.

Per-class coverage becomes available only after grant replay completes and closes again on an epoch change or continuity failure. It does **not** open global authorization/token/introspection or sign-in admission: account status and links remain uncovered and require independent reconstruction and operator attestation. Browser/on-behalf/runner sessions and rotations, delegation descendants/budgets/approvals, product-local PATs and other non-grant restrictions remain denied/gated in their owning authorities. Account coverage needs restriction producers and ordered restriction/lift custody; existing events alone do not establish it. The dormant consent flow still requires authoritative session and fence evidence.

External/offline access-token verifiers are not covered by operational entry checks. They need a separately approved epoch claim/verifier contract; external authorities must deny unproven recovery paths. This host does not claim to enforce them.

Local deterministic specifications and native adapter specifications are not an operational independence proof. A deployment restore rehearsal retaining the independent log is still required for the operational store, Chronicle, and both consumers together.

## Access-token and consent boundaries

Offline access JWT validation retains the accepted **630-second residual window** after revocation: a 600-second token lifetime plus 30 seconds of validation skew. A grant fence does not revoke a JWT already accepted by offline validation. Online refresh checks the primary grant authority on every attempt. The bounded access-token window is a contract, not a promise of immediate offline invalidation.

`GrantBoundRevocationFence` is conservative infrastructure for the dormant consent flow, not a new endpoint. A clear grant alone cannot prove session liveness or restore safety. Without authoritative session/recovery evidence it denies activation. It is not wired over the existing consent defaults; live consent integration remains separate.
